Beyond Borders, Beyond Graduation.

Every year, hundreds of thousands of international students travel to Australia to pursue higher education. What happens when they complete their degrees? An evidence led inquiry into employment, salary parity, and post-study reality.

01

INBOUND PATHWAYS & COHORTS

Australia's Global Student Community

Before we ask whether graduates find work, we need to see the scale and shape of the international student journey: where students come from, how large the cohort is, and what they study.

Australia's International Student Lifeline Runs Through Asia

Asia is the clear centre of gravity: China, India and Nepal contribute the largest flows, with every line converging on Australia. Hover a country to compare continuing students with new commencements.

Source: Australia Department of Education, International student numbers by country, by state and territory (2026) ↗.

Almost One in Three Students Is International

Almost one in three students in this higher-education cohort is international (30.7%). The square grid makes the proportion visible at a glance.

Source: Australia Department of Education, Key findings from the 2024 Higher Education Student Statistics ↗.

Business and Computing Anchor International Study

International enrolments are concentrated in business and management (36.0%) and computing and information systems (15.3%), before spreading across engineering, teaching and the sciences.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.
02

FROM STUDY TO WORK

The First Step After Graduation

Graduation reveals a difficult paradox: international students often perform worse than domestic peers when seeking full-time work in Australia, yet their outcomes are stronger when they work overseas after graduating. The charts below unpack this domestic disadvantage, show how it varies by discipline, and compare the employment and salary pathways that follow.

International Graduates Face a Persistent Employment Gap

The domestic advantage is broad rather than isolated: international graduates trail domestic graduates in most fields soon after completion, with the largest gaps in agriculture, business and engineering.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.

Discipline Shapes Pay and International Graduates Usually Earn Less

Starting pay depends on what graduates study, but it also differs by cohort: domestic graduates earn more than international graduates in most comparable disciplines.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.

For Many International Graduates, Employment Is Stronger Outside Australia

Unlike the previous chart, this small-multiples view follows destination: in 2025, every displayed study level has higher full-time employment outside Australia than in Australia. “Overseas” may include returning home or moving to another country.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.
03

PLACE MATTERS

Where Opportunity Is Concentrated

Outcomes are not evenly distributed across Australia. These maps connect the graduate story to place, showing where universities are located and how earnings vary between states and territories.

Where You Study Can Shape Your Early Career Outcomes

University-level outcomes are not identical: employability and median salary vary across institutions and locations. Use the focus selector to focus on a particular cluster and hover the symbols to compare the patterns. The chart shows associations, not that a university alone causes a particular outcome.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗

Graduate Earnings Vary Across the Map

Pay is geographically uneven. The darker states have higher median full-time weekly earnings, showing how the local labour market can shape the value of a graduate outcome.

Source: Australian Bureau of Statistics, Employee earnings, 2025 ↗
04

FIELD REALITIES & EXPERIENCE

One Outcome Never Tells the Whole Story

Employment and salary are only part of the picture. The final set of charts brings together employment intensity, pay, and satisfaction to show why a successful graduate experience cannot be reduced to one headline number.

Employment Outcomes Depend on What You Study

Medicine, pharmacy and rehabilitation sit among the strongest 2025 full-time outcomes, while communications, creative arts and psychology sit lower.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.

Higher Employment Does Not Always Mean Higher Pay

There is no single winning pathway: some countries combine higher employment with higher salaries, while others show that a job outcome can still come with lower starting pay. The dashed lines mark the cohort median reference points, about 52% full-time employment and a $70,000 median starting salary, countries can be compared as above or below the overall median.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.

Graduate Satisfaction Shifts Across Disciplines

Employment and pay are not the whole graduate experience. Follow each discipline across the years to see which fields rise, fall or remain consistently satisfied.

Source: QILT, 2025 Graduate Outcomes Survey ↗.

WHAT TO TAKE AWAY

International graduates contribute substantially to Australian higher education, but they face a clear disadvantage when entering the Australian workforce: domestic peers achieve higher full-time employment in most study areas. The gap is not universal, and many graduates achieve stronger full-time employment overseas, but outcomes still depend heavily on field of study, destination and local opportunity. Student success should therefore be measured not only by enrolment, but by whether graduates can access comparable opportunities after completing their degrees.
